Glossary

The shared vocabulary of the course. Every lesson uses these exact terms. Bookmark it — it grows as we go.

Business models — who does what

Fabless
A company that designs chips but owns no factory; it pays a foundry to manufacture them. Asset-light, high-margin, design-led. NVDA · AMD · AVGO · QCOM
Foundry
A contract manufacturer that fabricates chips designed by others. Capital-intensive; the leading edge is dominated by one player. TSM
IDM — Integrated Device Manufacturer
A company that both designs and manufactures its own chips in-house (the original model). INTC · MU · TXN · ADI
OSAT
Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test — the firms that package and test finished wafers into usable chips. The back end of manufacturing. ASE (US: ASX) · AMKR
EDA — Electronic Design Automation
The software used to design and verify chips. A tiny slice of industry revenue but an indispensable chokepoint; a tight duopoly. SNPS · CDNS
IP (Semiconductor IP)
Licensable, pre-designed circuit blocks (e.g. CPU cores) that designers drop into their chips rather than building from scratch. ARM
WFE — Wafer Fab Equipment
The machines that turn blank wafers into patterned chips. The "picks and shovels" of the industry. ASML · AMAT · LRCX · KLAC

The physical process

Wafer
A thin disc of ultra-pure silicon (usually 300 mm) on which hundreds of chips are fabricated at once, then cut apart.
Fab
A fabrication plant — the cleanroom factory where wafers are processed. A leading-edge fab now costs $20B+ to build.
Node / Process node
The generation of manufacturing technology, labelled in nanometres (e.g. "3 nm"). Smaller = newer, denser, more efficient — but the number is now a marketing label, not a literal measurement.
Leading edge vs. trailing edge
Leading edge = the newest, smallest nodes (today ~2–5 nm) for the most demanding chips. Trailing edge (a.k.a. mature/legacy, ~28 nm and older) = older nodes, still huge volume for cars, appliances, power. Moats differ sharply between them.
Lithography
The step that prints circuit patterns onto the wafer using light. The hardest, most expensive step — and the industry's tightest chokepoint.
EUV / DUV
Extreme Ultraviolet vs. Deep Ultraviolet lithography. EUV (13.5 nm light) is required for the most advanced nodes; ASML is the world's only maker of EUV machines. DUV is the older, still-essential workhorse.
Tape-out
The moment a finished chip design is sent to the foundry to be manufactured. The expensive point of no return for a design.
Process control (Inspection & Metrology)
Equipment that finds defects and measures features during fabrication. The hardest equipment category to copy. KLAC

Investing lens

Moat
A durable structural advantage that protects a company's profits from competition. The whole point of this course is judging whether one is real, widening, or eroding.
Chokepoint
A step in the value chain controlled by one or very few firms, that everyone else must pass through. The strongest source of a moat in this industry (e.g. EUV = ASML, leading-edge foundry = TSMC, EDA = SNPS+CDNS).
The semiconductor cycle
The industry's boom/bust rhythm: demand surges → capacity gets overbuilt → glut → prices crash → cutbacks → shortage → repeat. A cyclical peak can masquerade as a permanent moat — a core trap this course guards against.
Commodity (in chips)
A product where buyers choose mainly on price because supply is interchangeable (classically: DRAM/NAND memory). Thin moats, brutal cycles. MU
