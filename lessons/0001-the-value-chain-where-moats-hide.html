Lesson 01 · The Map

The Value Chain & Where Moats Hide

A chip passes through seven kinds of company before it ships. The single most useful thing you can know as an investor: moats are not spread evenly across them. Some links are near-monopolies. Some are commodities. Learn the map, and you already know where to hunt for gems.

Why start here

People say "I bought a semiconductor stock" as if that's one thing. It isn't. NVIDIA never touches a factory — it's fabless. TSMC never designs a product you'd recognise — it's a foundry. ASML doesn't make chips at all — it makes the machines that make chips. These are completely different businesses with completely different moats, all wearing the same "semis" label. (Dotted words like these are tap-to-define — try one.)

So before any company analysis, you need the map: who does what, and which links are protected. Get this, and a messy industry snaps into a short list of places worth your money.

1 · Design tools & IP — the blueprint kit

Chokepoint

Moat ●●●●● · SNPS · CDNS · ARM

EDA software and licensable IP blocks. Nobody can design a modern chip without them — yet it's a tiny slice of revenue with enormous leverage. A duopoly (Synopsys + Cadence) whose tools every design is locked into.

2 · Fabless design — the architects

Moat varies

Moat ●●●●● · NVDA · AMD · AVGO · QCOM

Design the chip, own no factory — manufacturing is outsourced to a foundry. The famous names live here. Moats range from strong (NVIDIA's software ecosystem) to thin (commodity designers). Strong, but more attackable than the tool/machine links.

3 · Foundry — the manufacturers-for-hire

Chokepoint

Moat ●●●●● · TSM

Take someone else's design and fabricate it at colossal scale. The leading edge is essentially one company — TSMC — and customers' designs get tuned to its specific process, making them costly to move.

4 · Equipment (WFE) — the picks & shovels

Chokepoint

Moat ●●●●● (litho) · ASML · AMAT · LRCX · KLAC

Build the machines a fab is made of. Each sub-step is dominated by one or two firms — and lithography is the purest monopoly in the whole industry: only ASML makes EUV machines. The widest moats live here.

5 · Materials — the consumables

Moat varies

Moat ●●●●● · (mostly non-US-listed)

Ultra-pure wafers, gases, chemicals, photoresist. Boring, essential, and often Japanese. Some niches (photoresist, polished wafers) are quietly concentrated; others are competitive.

6 · OSAT — assembly & test

Contested

Moat ●●●●● · ASX · AMKR

Package the finished wafer into a usable chip and test it — the back end. More commoditised, lower margin, more competitors. (Watch this space: advanced packaging for AI chips is making parts of it far more valuable.)

7 · IDMs — the do-it-all-in-house

Moat varies

Moat ●●●●● · INTC · TXN · ADI · MU

Design and manufacture under one roof — the original model. Quality depends on the niche: analog IDMs (TXN, ADI) are durable; memory IDMs (MU) ride a brutal commodity cycle; Intel is fighting to keep its manufacturing edge.

Read the dots and a strategy writes itself. The widest, most durable moats cluster in the "tools & machines" links nobody talks about at parties — EDA, lithography, process control, leading-edge foundry — not necessarily in the headline chip brands. That single insight is why investors quietly love ASML and TSMC.

◆ Moat Lens

When you hear a semiconductor stock pitched, your first question is now: which link of the chain is this, and how protected is that link? A wonderful company in a commodity link (great memory maker) can still be a poor long-term hold. A merely good company sitting on a chokepoint can be a gem. Position in the chain often matters more than the company itself.

Check yourself

Don't peek at the table — pull these from memory. That effort is what makes it stick.

Q1

Which company is the world's only maker of EUV lithography machines — the purest single-firm chokepoint in the whole chain?

ASML. No EUV machine means no leading-edge chips — and no one else on Earth makes one. TSMC and NVIDIA are powerful, but they are customers of this chokepoint, not the chokepoint itself.

Q2

A "fabless" company like NVIDIA is best described as one that —

Fabless = design only, manufacturing outsourced to a foundry (usually TSMC). Owning factories and designs is the IDM model (Intel). Building the machines is equipment (ASML). See Glossary → Fabless.

Q3

Which link tends to have the thinnest, most cyclical moat — where buyers choose mostly on price?

Commodity DRAM/NAND memory. Supply is interchangeable, so it competes on price and swings violently with the cycle — the opposite of a chokepoint. The other three are among the strongest moats in the industry.

The one idea to keep

If you remember nothing else: a chip's journey has seven links, and the moats live mostly in the unglamorous "tools & machines" links — EDA, lithography, process control, leading-edge foundry — far more than in the famous brands.
